Cookies and analytics

This website uses Google Analytics 4, a service provided by Google, to understand how visitors find and use the site — for example the pages viewed, how a visit arrived, device and browser type, and approximate (city-level) location. Google Analytics sets first-party cookies such as _ga and sends this usage data to Google, which processes it as described in how Google uses information from sites that use its services. We use these reports only to measure and improve the website, and we do not send names, email addresses, phone numbers or other directly identifying details to Google Analytics.

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, the Google tag is switched off and no analytics data is sent. You can also block or delete cookies in your browser settings, or install the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.

Separately, the site records aggregate, cookie-free first-party events (such as button clicks and form submissions) to measure enquiries. Infrastructure providers may process limited technical information necessary to deliver and secure the website.

Account registration

When you create a customer account, we store the name and email address you provide, a one-way derived password hash with a unique salt, account timestamps, and server-side session records needed to keep you signed in. We do not need or intend to store plaintext passwords. Authentication cookies are used to maintain signed-in sessions.

Orders and enquiries

When you submit an order or enquiry, we may store the information needed to process it, including your name, email address, phone number, delivery address, order items, order value and request status. This information is used for fulfilment, customer support, business records and applicable legal obligations.

Community activity

Community posts, comments and display names are public. We store the content you publish, engagement records such as likes, basic activity counts and reports submitted for moderation. Do not post passwords, payment credentials, private identity documents or another person’s private information.

Email OTP and social sign-in

If you use email OTP, we process your email address and temporary verification-code records needed to complete sign-in. If you use Google, GitHub, Facebook or X sign-in, the provider may send us an account identifier, display name and—where the provider makes it available—an email address. Provider access tokens are used only to retrieve the sign-in profile during authentication and are not intentionally stored as long-term account credentials.
